DREAMGRAPH ENGINE ALIGNMENT — PROPOSED RESOLUTIONS TO THE NERVOUS POINTS

These are proposed policies and acceptance criteria, not claims about what
the implementation already guarantees. Incorporate them into the existing
plan and its governed slices rather than starting another architecture.

The objective is to preserve DreamGraph's living, self-adjusting cognitive
engine while making its knowledge, state transitions and operating costs
trustworthy across every surface.


1. MODEL PROFILES FOR FULL SCANS VERSUS ESTABLISHED-GRAPH WORK

Decision:
Separate the choice of model from the meaning and completeness of the work.
Full scanning, targeted enrichment, dreaming and normalization may use
different user-selected profiles, but must retain their respective evidence
and output contracts.

GPT-4.1 and GPT-5.4 in the original nervous point should remain examples or
saved preferences, not permanent architectural defaults. The user has already
changed scan models in response to actual cost.

Required behavior:
- Show the effective provider, model, reasoning configuration, hop depth and
  relevant limits before starting an expensive job.
- Preserve explicit user choices. Do not escalate to a stronger or more
  expensive model silently, including through a fallback path.
- Treat max-hops as a traversal-depth control, not a spending guarantee.
  A one-hop neighborhood around a large hub may still be expensive.
- Bound context expansion separately where configured: selected neighbors,
  input tokens, output allowance, retries and cumulative job spend.
- Record omitted context and the selection policy. A bounded neighborhood
  must not be presented as the complete graph.
- Record the effective enrichment configuration with each result/checkpoint.
  Changing it must not silently relabel earlier results as newly enriched.
- Preserve completed source scanning and valid enrichment when a budget is
  exhausted. Expose which nodes remain evidence-only and support targeted
  continuation rather than requiring another full scan.

Do not weaken the current completeness requirement for automatic dreaming
merely to make a budget-limited scan appear successful. Any change to that
policy needs an explicit decision.

Acceptance:
A stopped enrichment run retains its useful work, reports its remaining
coverage accurately, and can continue under an explicitly selected profile.
No operator must discover the actual model or cost policy from the bill.


2. A STRONGER DREAMER CAN GROW POOR EVIDENCE FASTER

Decision:
Evaluate stronger models by the quality of their supported conclusions and
useful hypotheses, not by fluency, confidence, output volume or graph growth.

DreamGraph must still be allowed to dream. Novel ideas need not already be
proven, but they must remain distinguishable from established knowledge.

Required behavior:
- Keep generated hypotheses separate from evidence-backed assertions.
- Test models against frozen, representative tasks with reviewed evidence:
  architecture retrieval, dependency reasoning, stale-source detection,
  contradictions, missing evidence and genuinely useful proposed changes.
- Include cases where the correct response is uncertainty, abstention or a
  request for more evidence.
- Measure unsupported promotions, missed contradictions, evidence accuracy
  and downstream usefulness alongside cost.
- Do not count repeated paraphrases, repeated model passes or agreement
  between models as independent corroboration.
- Preserve the ability to revisit a conclusion when its supporting source
  changes or is withdrawn.

Acceptance:
A more persuasive answer with the same weak evidence cannot acquire a
stronger trust status merely because a more capable model produced it.
Conversely, useful speculative ideas remain available as hypotheses rather
than being discarded because they are not yet facts.


3. RETRIEVAL, MUTATION AND BACKGROUND-JOB CONTRACTS

Decision:
Establish common guarantees beneath the surfaces, without requiring every
transport to have an identical presentation.

Retrieval:
Every result must communicate its scope, relevant revision/freshness,
completeness and uncertainty. Distinguish:
- complete for the requested scope;
- partial because of a limit, permission or unavailable source;
- stale or awaiting reconciliation;
- genuinely empty.

Bound large responses and provide continuation through the existing
retrieval mechanisms. Pagination or continuation must either preserve the
relevant revision or explicitly disclose that it changed. Never silently
drop nodes, edge types or provenance to fit a response.

Do not require an expensive global count on every request merely to display
a completeness label. Report what is actually known.

Mutation:
Return success only after the authoritative change is durably committed.
Use existing operation identity, revision checks and recovery facilities.
A retry after a lost reply must not duplicate the operation.

Where persistence spans files, database state or external effects, define
the recovery sequence explicitly. Do not promise universal exactly-once
execution or rollback of effects that cannot actually be reversed.

Background jobs:
Give each job an owner, cancellation state, checkpoint policy and bounded
resource use. Account for outstanding parallel calls when enforcing a
budget; checking spend only after completion is insufficient.

Acceptance:
Dropped responses, client refreshes, process crashes and concurrent clients
cannot turn a partial read into a complete one, an uncommitted write into a
success, or an already committed operation into an accidental duplicate.


4. EXISTING CLIENTS ASSUMING UNBOUNDED JSON OR SHARED BROWSER STATE

Decision:
Change transport and session contracts explicitly. Browser state must not
become an accidental authority for engine state.

Required behavior:
- Inventory consumers that assume a response is one exhaustive JSON object.
- Introduce bounded responses and continuation through negotiated or
  versioned contracts, with adapters where needed.
- Test that clients follow continuation and preserve its revision semantics.
- Make each client able to reconstruct its view from authoritative state
  after reconnect, refresh or context loss.
- A browser refresh should not implicitly cancel a durable daemon-owned job.
  Session-bound work may stop, but that distinction must be explicit.
- Keep human UI state separate from execution authority. An open tab, a
  selected plan or a cached badge is not proof of the current job state.
- Preserve the separation between native API continuation and CLI execution;
  do not impose API-only envelopes on a CLI adapter.

Acceptance:
Two clients observing the same engine converge on the same authoritative
state despite different local caches. A client that cannot understand a new
response contract fails visibly instead of treating partial data as complete.


5. GLOBAL COVERAGE PERCENTAGES ENCOURAGING SHALLOW TESTS

Decision:
Use percentages as summaries, never as substitutes for demonstrated behavior.

Required behavior:
Build a requirements-to-evidence matrix covering:
- important state transitions;
- graph types and relationship types;
- adapter and surface contracts;
- stale, incomplete and contradictory evidence;
- persistence and recovery failures;
- concurrency, cancellation and resource limits.

For agent usefulness, include representative tasks from DreamGraph and
web64-react separately. Success should require the right information and
provenance, not merely a syntactically valid response or enough returned nodes.

Keep structural coverage, semantic-enrichment coverage, evidence quality and
task usefulness separate. Evidence-only fallback is not successful LLM
enrichment, although it can still be valid and useful structural evidence.

Reuse focused tests for distinct behavior. Do not multiply unchanged global
suites across every model, surface or configuration.

Acceptance:
Every important requirement has an identifiable test or an explicit unverified
status. A headline percentage cannot conceal an untested graph class,
recovery path or agent workflow.


6. API CAPABILITIES VERSUS CLI IDENTIFIERS AND MODEL OVERRIDES

Decision:
Keep model identity, transport capability and cost policy separate.

Required behavior:
- Distinguish requested model, resolved model and actual execution transport.
- Maintain API parameter/schema capabilities separately from CLI-supported
  identifiers, effort settings and output mechanisms.
- Do not infer temperature support, structured-output guarantees or context
  limits from a model's name.
- Validate explicit overrides against the relevant adapter contract. Preserve
  supported custom-model workflows rather than hard-coding one vendor list.
- Unsupported settings produce actionable feedback. Do not silently discard
  them or substitute a different model.
- Retain host-side output validation where required. A CLI producing valid
  JSON is not automatically equivalent to API-enforced schema output.
- Report fallback use explicitly, including its reason and any changed
  evidence, capability or cost guarantees.
- Do not apply API token tariffs to subscription CLI usage and label the
  result as an actual charge. Keep measured cost, estimated cost and unknown
  subscription consumption distinct.

Acceptance:
Each execution can be traced to its real model and adapter. No fallback or
override silently changes spending or weakens the required output contract.


7. LEGACY GRAPHS WITHOUT RELIABLE SCAN OR CHECKPOINT BASELINES

Decision:
Treat absent historical evidence as unknown, not as permission to invent a
baseline or rebuild the graph destructively.

Required behavior:
- Inspect schema, stable identities, source references, revisions, checkpoints
  and available provenance before selecting a repair strategy.
- Separate known-format migration from evidence reconstruction and semantic
  re-enrichment. They are different operations.
- Produce a read-only report of what is recoverable, ambiguous, orphaned,
  stale or missing.
- Preserve historical enrichment and human contributions with their original
  provenance. A new scan must not manufacture an old verification history.
- Preview repair changes and identity mappings before applying them.
- Preserve a recoverable baseline and test restoration.
- Stage expensive re-enrichment separately from structural repair so a
  depleted API balance does not leave a half-migrated persistent format.

Acceptance:
A legacy graph can be inspected and repaired without pretending its missing
history exists. Interrupted repair leaves a recoverable state, and ambiguous
records are surfaced rather than silently deleted or reassigned.


8. INDEPENDENT PROJECTIONS DEPENDING ON OLD IDS AND CONFIDENCE MEANINGS

Decision:
Create one canonical meaning for shared data; adapt each surface to it.
Do not attempt to align eight surfaces through eight separate rewrites.

Required behavior:
- Map each shared concept to its existing engine owner, persisted
  representation, allowed writers and consumers.
- Preserve stable identities independently of display names and UI grouping.
- Document what confidence means wherever it is exposed. Separate model
  confidence, evidence strength, freshness and validation status.
- Introduce adapters from legacy representations to the canonical contract
  where needed. Explicitly flag lossy conversions.
- Compare old and new projections against the same graph revision and scope.
  Normalize irrelevant presentation differences before comparison.
- Test all relevant node/edge classes, permissions, omissions, provenance and
  continuation—not just fields common to every surface.
- Include plugin and federation boundaries where they consume these records.

Acceptance:
MCP, CLI, Architect, Explorer and analytics may present different views, but
cannot contradict one another about identity, evidence or state because
they independently interpreted the same field.


9. PAID CANARIES, SPEND CAPS AND PROVIDER RETENTION

Decision:
Ordinary CI makes zero paid model requests. Real-provider evaluation is a
separately authorized activity with explicit data and budget scope.

Required behavior:
- Default to deterministic fixtures and existing offline test mechanisms.
- Require opt-in credentials, approved models, declared test data and a
  configured monetary limit before paid evaluation.
- No configured budget means no paid run, not unlimited spending.
- Reserve a conservative maximum cost before issuing each request, including
  concurrent calls and retries. Reconcile against returned usage afterward.
- Unknown price is unknown—not zero. Do not automatically admit a paid
  request that cannot be bounded under the chosen policy.
- Record pricing provenance and distinguish estimates from final charges.
- Disclose exactly what source/evidence is sent to a provider.
- Configure supported retention/storage settings explicitly and state their
  limits. Never claim zero retention merely because a client flag was set.
- Sanitize saved test artifacts, logs and reports; preserve no credentials.

Acceptance:
A normal test run cannot consume API credit. An opted-in run cannot silently
escalate its model, retry past its authorized allocation or send additional
private evidence beyond the declared scope.


10. NO UNREVIEWED LEGACY GRAPH REWRITE

Decision:
A migration is a controlled state transition, not a cleanup command.

Required behavior:
- Bind approval to a specific migration plan, input revision and change scope.
- Keep a backup and test the restoration procedure before destructive work.
- Produce a delta preview covering identities, relationships, trust states,
  deletions and unresolved mappings—not just row counts.
- Use staged migration where practical, then perform a controlled cutover.
- Coordinate with active scans, enrichment jobs and other writers. Pause,
  fence or reject incompatible writes using the existing lifecycle authority.
- Detect stale operations prepared against the old revision.
- Define what happens to writes after cutover. Restoring an old backup must
  not silently discard legitimate newer work.
- Use an explicit maintenance/write barrier where safe online reconciliation
  is not already supported; do not invent live migration to avoid a pause.

Acceptance:
Crash, cancellation, stale-client and concurrent-scan tests demonstrate that
the graph cannot be left ambiguously half-migrated. Rollback limitations and
the treatment of post-cutover writes are documented and tested.


11. SOURCE FACTS, HUMAN ASSERTIONS, HYPOTHESES AND VALIDATED INSIGHTS

Decision:
Preserve both provenance and trust state. They are related but not
interchangeable dimensions.

Required behavior:
- Retain whether a claim originated from source evidence, a human assertion,
  a model hypothesis or a documented validation process.
- Preserve the actual supporting references, their revisions and the scope
  of what they support.
- Do not automatically treat a human assertion as verified, or a
  source-backed statement as permanently current.
- An insight's promotion records what validation occurred and what evidence
  supports the resulting status.
- Repetition, narration, metacognitive review or federation must not launder
  an assertion into independent evidence.
- Track shared evidence ancestry so two derived claims quoting the same
  source are not counted as two independent confirmations.
- Surface contradictions instead of resolving them solely by model
  confidence or majority vote.
- On evidence change, mark affected claims for re-evaluation without erasing
  their history.

Acceptance:
The same unsupported claim cannot become validated by circulating through
dreaming, narration, normalization and federation. Creative hypotheses
remain useful, discoverable hypotheses until their evidence changes.


12. PRESERVE THE COMPACT UI WHILE IMPROVING UNDERSTANDING

Decision:
Improve observability and control without undoing the current compact,
desktop-tool visual direction.

Required behavior:
- Keep dense layouts, restrained surfaces, compact toolbars and contextual
  inspectors.
- Use unobtrusive text/link-style controls for secondary navigation and
  reveal actions, while retaining correct keyboard and interaction behavior.
- Reserve stronger visual controls for primary actions and consequential
  changes. Do not turn every detail into a bordered button.
- Make freshness, completeness, active execution, recovery state and effective
  model configuration easy to inspect without permanent visual clutter.
- Expose the evidence behind health, weather, confidence and similar
  summaries. Preserve DreamGraph's vocabulary, but make it explainable.
- Distinguish saved configuration from effective running configuration.
- Keep full details accessible through contextual inspection; compact must
  not mean silently omitted.
- Test realistic dense graphs, long labels, narrow windows, reconnects and
  partial states—not only attractive empty views.

Acceptance:
A user can determine what is running, what knowledge is incomplete, why an
action is blocked and what control will change it. Visual polish must not
conceal uncertainty or substitute for functional status.


13. REUSE THE EXISTING ENGINE INSTEAD OF BUILDING ANOTHER ONE

Decision:
Align existing trust-state, evidence-ledger, lifecycle, coverage and provider
modules. Introduce a new subsystem only for a demonstrated responsibility
that no current owner can reasonably fulfill.

Required behavior:
- Begin each change with the owning module and contract it repairs.
- Prefer extending existing APIs and adding adapters over creating competing
  registries, schedulers, ledgers, evaluators or persistence authorities.
- Keep transport/UI concerns outside cognitive policy and evidence ownership.
- Fit work into the current governed slices. Close a useful end-to-end path
  before broadening the change across every surface.
- Preserve working behavior through baseline fixtures and focused conformance
  checks. Correctness defects may require intentional behavior changes, but
  those changes must be named rather than hidden as refactoring.
- Remove old paths only after their consumers have migrated and their
  compatibility obligations have an explicit disposition.
- Keep audit findings traceable through implementation, tests and migration
  evidence. Do not mark a finding resolved because a replacement file exists.
- Retain living-engine behavior: targeted and lucid dreams, intervention,
  adaptation, temporal/causal reasoning and federation must not be flattened
  into a static repository index merely because that is easier to standardize.

Acceptance:
The overhaul reduces duplicated meaning and makes existing capabilities
compose reliably. It does not leave a new canonical engine alongside an
unchanged legacy engine with a third layer keeping them synchronized.


OVERALL PERSPECTIVE

DreamGraph's coherence should come from explicit ownership, evidence and
lifecycle contracts—not from requiring one maintainer, one model or one huge
context window to remember every interaction.

Be strict where mistakes damage trust, persistent state, privacy or budgets.
Remain flexible where DreamGraph is intentionally exploratory.

The finished system should let an agent:
- discover the relevant knowledge without silently losing important context;
- distinguish known, inferred, disputed, stale and incomplete information;
- act through the proper authority and verify what actually committed;
- continue safely after interruption;
- understand the limits and cost of further work.

That is the alignment target: preserve the original ambition while making
the engine and its surfaces dependable enough to sustain it.
